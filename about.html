About the Association

Why the Springfield Waste Management Association exists, what it takes care of, and how it is governed.

Why the association exists

Springfield Estates was developed in the 1980s with shared on-site sewerage systems instead of a connection to city sewer. Some lots could not support their own drainfield, so homes were grouped onto shared systems (Systems A through M) that carry wastewater to community drainfields. Because these systems serve multiple homes at once, an association was established to manage them collectively, so every connected home shares both the responsibility and the benefit of proper upkeep.

For a number of years the association was inactive, but the shared systems never stopped needing care. In 2026, homeowners came together to restore it, electing a volunteer Board of Directors in October.

Source: Supplemental Declaration (AF# 8510180315) and Amendment 2 to the Sewerage Contract (AF# 8608220247), recorded with Pierce County.

What the association takes care of

In plain terms, here’s what the association is responsible for.

  • Routine inspection of pump stations and shared equipment
  • Maintenance of the community drainfields
  • Scheduled septic tank pumping, on a regular rotation, for every connected home
  • Repairs and replacement when shared equipment breaks down or wears out
  • Recordkeeping and reporting required by Pierce County
  • Collecting assessments and paying for the services that keep the systems running
  • Filings that keep the association in good standing

Source: Contract for Maintenance and Operation of On-Site Sewerage Systems (AF# 8510180314), Sections 14 and 15.

As the board completes its review of the systems, this list will be updated.

What the association is not

The Springfield Waste Management Association is not a homeowners association. Its authority comes from recorded documents specific to the shared sewerage systems and is limited to them. It has no say over home appearance, landscaping, paint colors, fences, or anything else an HOA typically governs.

Source: Supplemental Declaration (AF# 8510180315) and Sewerage Contract (AF# 8510180314).

How the association is governed

The association is governed by a volunteer Board of Directors elected by member homeowners. Directors currently serve one-year terms, and the board selects its officers (President, Secretary, and Treasurer) from among its directors. The board meets monthly on Zoom, and meeting minutes are posted once approved.

Common questions

Is my home a member?

119 homes are members under the recorded documents (Amendment 2, Exhibit A). Use Look up my home & system on the homepage to check yours.

Does anything change with my home’s plumbing?

No. This is about who maintains the shared equipment (pump stations, drainfields, transmission lines) and how that cost is shared fairly across every home that uses it.

Do I have to pay anything right now?

No. Assessments come only after the board reviews the systems and builds a budget, and that budget will be presented to homeowners for approval first. You can follow that progress in Our Path Forward.

How can I reach the board or attend a meeting?

Email the association at springfieldestateswma@gmail.com, or join a board meeting on the second Monday of each month at 6:00 PM on Zoom. Meeting details are on the homepage.
