What this is, in plain terms

Springfield Estates was built in 1985 with shared on-site sewerage systems instead of a connection to city sewer. Because these systems serve multiple homes at once, an association was established to manage them collectively — inspecting equipment, preventive maintenance, scheduling pumping, and handling repairs — so every connected home shares both the responsibility and the peace of mind of proper upkeep.

For years, the association was abandoned — no board, no dues collected, no communication to anyone, and no repairs or services. If this is the first you're hearing about any of this, you're not alone; most of the neighborhood hasn't heard from the association in a long time. But the shared systems never stopped needing care, and the structure to manage them together never disappeared — it just needs an organization willing to step into it again. That's what this meeting is for: hearing from the neighborhood, and electing directors to properly reinstate the association.

Nothing about your home's plumbing or septic system changes because of this. This is about who maintains the shared equipment — pump stations, drainfields, transmission lines — and how that gets paid for, fairly, across every home that uses it.

What the association takes care of

In plain terms, here's what actually gets done.

  • Routine inspection of pump stations and shared equipment
  • Scheduled septic tank pumping, on a regular rotation, for every connected home
  • Repairs and replacement when equipment breaks down or wears out
  • Recordkeeping required by Pierce County Environmental Health
  • Collecting dues and paying the people who do the actual work
  • Filing and reporting so the association stays in good standing
This is not, and cannot become, a homeowners association. The Springfield Waste Management Association's authority is limited to the shared on-site sewerage systems — Systems A through M. It has no say over home appearance, landscaping, paint colors, fences, or anything else an HOA typically governs, and it cannot expand into one. This is, and will only ever be, about keeping the shared sewerage systems working for everyone connected to them.

Common questions

Why is this happening now?

The shared systems never stopped needing care, even during the years the association was inactive. A neighbor stepped up to help restart the process — but it only works with the whole neighborhood's involvement.

Who can I talk to with questions before the meeting?

Reach out any time — contact information is below.

Is this an HOA?

No. This is a waste management association only. Its authority comes from recorded documents specific to the shared sewerage systems — not general property or architectural control like a homeowners association has.

Could this become an HOA in the future?

No. The association's authority is legally limited to the shared sewerage systems (Systems A–M) and cannot expand beyond that — not by a vote, and not by a future board.

Do I have to pay anything right now?

No. This meeting is about hearing from homeowners and electing directors. Any assessment amount would go through its own separate, properly noticed process before anyone owes anything.
